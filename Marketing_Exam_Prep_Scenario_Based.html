Marketing Exam Prep: Scenario-Based Questions

Only scenario-based questions will be asked. This guide turns every major topic from your notes into exam-style scenarios with diagnosis, framework, recommended moves and risks. Fourteen scenarios are drawn from real Pakistani headlines around November 2026 (led by Dar-e-Nijaat); the rest are practice cases that cover the remaining topics.

How to use this in the exam: Read the scenario once. Name the concept/framework first, then diagnose the problem, recommend 2–3 concrete moves, and flag one risk. Keep answers tight (3–5 sentences).
Viral · Eras

1. Views Versus Value

Scenario

Dar-e-Nijaat has crossed 1.7 billion social media views in 23 episodes and its producer celebrates 16.5 TRPs. A rival executive claims this proves Pakistani TV has reached the Marketing Era. Is he right?

Diagnosis

Views and TRPs are volume metrics. They belong to Sales-Era scorekeeping, not Marketing-Era customer-value tracking.

Framework

Production Era → make it efficiently. Sales Era → sell hard and measure units/TRPs. Marketing Era → understand needs first, then measure retention, lifetime value and share of wallet.

Recommended Moves

Track episode-to-episode retention, ARY Plus sign-ups, repeat viewing and OST streams per viewer instead of celebrating raw reach alone.

Risk

Viral reach can hide whether fans will stay for the next drama.

Viral · Personalization

2. The Playlist Question

Scenario

The OST “Alvida Alvida” topped Spotify’s Made in Pakistan OSTs playlist and reached number 7 on the Top 50 Pakistan chart. That playlist is editorially curated, while Spotify also tailors songs for each listener. A music manager must decide how to use both.

Diagnosis

Editorial curation is firm-led; Discover-style tailoring is data-led personalization.

Framework

Customization = customer chooses options. Customerisation = company and customer co-create. Personalization = company silently tailors using data.

Recommended Moves

Pitch the track to editors for reach, and invite fans to submit covers and remixes for co-creation (customerisation).

Risk

Over-targeting without transparency can feel creepy.

Viral · Purple Cow

3. The Borrowed Buzz

Scenario

Fans organised a Dar-e-Nijaat watch party at a Delhi café and screening nights are being held in Hyderabad. Nobody paid for the events, yet clips spread widely. A Karachi café owner wants the same buzz.

Diagnosis

The drama is remarkable, so people remark on it for free.

Framework

Godin’s Purple Cow: being merely “good” is invisible (brown cow). Being remarkable gets talked about. Organizations often behave like cautious baboons — copying each other and avoiding risk.

Recommended Moves

Host themed viewing nights for hit shows with matching menu items and a shareable photo corner so the café itself becomes remarkable.

Risk

Borrowed buzz fades fast; screenings may raise licensing questions.

Viral · Competition Map

4. Beyond the Channel

Scenario

Dar-e-Nijaat airs Fridays and Saturdays at 8 pm on ARY Digital, streams on ARY Plus and can also be watched on YouTube. A rival channel benchmarks only other channels’ prime-time slots. Its viewers meanwhile spend evenings on reels, games and YouTube.

Diagnosis

The channel tracks direct competitors but ignores alternatives and substitutes.

Framework

Competitor = similar choice. Alternative = another option that meets the same need. Substitute = satisfies the need in a completely different way (e.g., short video instead of a drama).

Recommended Moves

Define the need as “an emotional evening escape” and benchmark against every option, including short video and gaming.

Risk

Free online availability can cannibalize TV and app revenue.

Viral · Pricing

5. The Fuel Shock

Scenario

Petrol rose to Rs323 per litre from 20 November. A Karachi delivery startup that uses cost-plus pricing must decide whether to raise fees. Rival apps are silent and customers are already angry.

Diagnosis

Cost-plus simply passes cost to customers and ignores perceived value.

Framework

Price is the only P that earns revenue. Cost-plus is safe for accountants, risky for marketers. Other tools: freemium, skimming, penetration, decoy, psychological pricing.

Recommended Moves

Add a small, transparent fuel surcharge and offer a subscription with free delivery to reward loyal users.

Risk

Hidden fees cause backlash; a price war can erase margins.

Viral · Anchoring

6. The Halo Car

Scenario

A Toyota Land Cruiser 300 Hybrid has arrived in Pakistan with an Rs11 crore price tag, while seven imported models (including the Raize and Note) are getting cheaper. A dealer displays them together. Buyers suddenly see mid-range SUVs as bargains.

Diagnosis

The Land Cruiser works as a price anchor.

Framework

Anchoring and psychological pricing shape perceived value. A decoy option makes another choice look better.

Recommended Moves

Feature the halo car prominently, then present a clearly better-value mid trim next to a weaker one.

Risk

If the comparison is misleading, trust erodes.

Viral · Channels

7. When the Internet Slows

Scenario

An undersea cable fault is slowing internet across Pakistan, with repairs delayed for about two more weeks. An online clothing brand sees failed checkouts and abandoned carts. Its physical outlets sit nearly unused.

Diagnosis

A pure-click channel is hostage to infrastructure.

Framework

Brick-and-mortar = physical only. Pure-click = online only. Brick-and-click combines both and lets you control the last mile of experience.

Recommended Moves

Add WhatsApp ordering, cash on delivery, in-store pickup and a lightweight mobile site.

Risk

Inconsistent prices across channels create conflict.

Viral · Promotion

8. Free Fame

Scenario

A creator reimagined Dar-e-Nijaat as an Ekta Kapoor production, and Indian journalist Ravish Kumar’s remarks on the drama circulated widely. ARY paid for neither. The marketing head wonders whether to ignore, claim or fuel such content.

Diagnosis

This is earned media and community-driven promotion.

Framework

Promotion tools: advertising (paid), brand placement, activation (live experience), publicity, sales promotion, public relations, personal selling. Modern tools win by inviting participation instead of demanding attention.

Recommended Moves

Credit and share the best fan edits; create moments designed to be remixed.

Risk

Over-control kills authenticity; cross-border politics can hijack the message.

Viral · Services

9. The Dirty Ward

Scenario

Reports of cockroaches in a patient ward at Lahore General Hospital sparked outrage. A private hospital group wants to advertise itself as the clean, caring alternative. Its ads are ready, but its staff training is not.

Diagnosis

Trust in healthcare is judged through visible evidence and staff behaviour.

Framework

The extra 3 Ps of services: People (who deliver), Process (steps of delivery), Physical Evidence (visible clues customers use to judge quality).

Recommended Moves

Train staff first, publish cleanliness audits and show real wards in the ads.

Risk

Over-promising means any lapse will go viral.

Viral · Post-Purchase

10. The Price-Drop Regret

Scenario

Seven imported models, including the Toyota Raize and Nissan Note, are reported to be getting cheaper in Pakistan. A dealer fears recent buyers will feel cheated. Some are already asking for partial refunds.

Diagnosis

This is buyer’s remorse inside the critical first 30 days.

Framework

The sale is the starting gun. Post-purchase activities (follow-ups at 3 and 30 days, warranties, returns, support) decide loyalty.

Recommended Moves

Call buyers first, offer free servicing or accessories, and publish a clear goodwill policy.

Risk

Blanket refunds set a costly precedent.

Viral · Buying Center

11. The Solar Decision

Scenario

Electricity prices may rise by Rs1.34 per unit for three months, so a Lahore university considers rooftop solar. The registrar raised the idea, engineers set technical needs, and the finance director controls the budget. A solar vendor pitches only the registrar.

Diagnosis

The vendor addressed only one role in a multi-role decision unit.

Framework

Five buying roles: Initiator (suggests), Influencer (advises), Decision Maker (final choice), Buyer (pays), User/Consumer (uses). One person can play several roles.

Recommended Moves

Give engineers technical specs, finance a payback table, and the registrar a campus-wide savings story.

Risk

Roles shift between organisations — remap each account.

Viral · Segmentation

12. The Gold Rush

Scenario

Gold rates climbed to Rs384,136 after a big jump. A jeweller segments buyers only by age and city. In reality, some are wedding shoppers, some investors and some occasional gift buyers.

Diagnosis

The segments do not change any decision.

Framework

Four bases: Demographic, Geographic, Psychographic, Behavioural. Behavioural segmentation (usage, loyalty, readiness to buy) is the most powerful for driving action.

Recommended Moves

Offer installment plans for wedding buyers, price alerts for investors and gift catalogues for occasional shoppers.

Risk

Pushing speculative buying onto vulnerable customers harms trust.

Viral · PLC & Adopters

13. The Sky Internet

Scenario

Pakistan’s satellite internet rules are reportedly almost ready. A reseller expects instant mass demand. Today only remote businesses and tech enthusiasts show interest.

Diagnosis

This is the Introduction stage with innovators, not the early majority.

Framework

PLC stages: Introduction → Growth → Maturity → Decline. Adopter groups: Innovators, Early Adopters, Early Majority, Late Majority, Laggards. Strategy must match both stage and adopter type.

Recommended Moves

Target remote offices, NGOs and tech users first, then build proof through case studies.

Risk

Regulation delays and high prices could stall growth.

Viral · Positioning

14. Spiritual Romance

Scenario

Dar-e-Nijaat, written by Umera Ahmed, is known as a spiritual romantic drama about heartbreak, forgiveness and redemption. Rival channels launch glamorous romances with similar casts. A new channel asks how to position itself.

Diagnosis

Copying romance makes the channel wallpaper beside the owners of that belief.

Framework

Positioning is the place a brand holds in the customer’s mind relative to competitors. Good positioning is clear, relevant, credible and different. It is what customers believe, not what you say.

Recommended Moves

Own a different belief (family comedy, mystery, social realism) and keep every show consistent with it.

Risk

Claiming every genre means audiences believe nothing.

Practice · Mortality

15. The DVD Shop

Scenario

A Rawalpindi DVD rental shop earns steady but falling income. The owner refuses change because the shop supported his family for twenty years. A nearby café has begun offering movie nights.

Diagnosis

This is denial of decline.

Framework

Law of Mortality: every product, brand or business eventually loses relevance unless it adapts. Options are harvest, revive or exit.

Recommended Moves

Harvest rentals for cash while launching a private-screening and gaming lounge.

Risk

Waiting leaves only emergency options.

Practice · Habit

16. The Chai Loop

Scenario

A loose-tea brand has loyal households that reorder without thinking. A new premium tea offers free sachets and a discount. Few families switch.

Diagnosis

Buyers are repeating habit, not re-evaluating.

Framework

Interrupting an automatic buying loop is harder than building one. Habitual buying is a form of behavioural loyalty.

Recommended Moves

Use door-to-door sampling, strong shelf-eye placement and a trigger such as a taste challenge.

Risk

Discounting alone invites one-time trial without loyalty.

Viral · Ansoff

17. Crossing Borders

Scenario

Dar-e-Nijaat is spreading to Indian audiences through YouTube, fan screenings and social clips, even though Pakistani shows are reported banned there. Producer Three’s Entertainment owns the existing product and has found a new audience. Investors now suggest a global streaming platform.

Diagnosis

Reaching India is market development; building a platform is diversification.

Framework

Ansoff Matrix: Market Penetration (existing product + existing market), Market Development (existing product + new market), Product Development (new product + existing market), Diversification (new product + new market — riskiest).

Recommended Moves

Pursue subtitled, legal releases in diaspora markets and keep deepening the home market first.

Risk

Legal limits and platform costs could drain focus.

Practice · Image & Relationships

18. The Festival Sale Trap

Scenario

A Karachi clothing brand runs heavy Eid discounts every year and posts record sales. After the festival, most customers disappear until the next sale. The owner wonders why loyalty is so low despite high revenue.

Diagnosis

The brand is strong on sales but weak on relationships and image beyond price.

Framework

Image = the impression the brand creates in customers’ minds. Sales = activities that convert and earn revenue. Relationship marketing = building long-term trust and loyalty. High sales without relationships produce one-time buyers who leave when a rival appears.

Recommended Moves

Shift some budget from pure discounts into membership benefits, post-purchase care and consistent quality messaging so image and relationships catch up with sales.

Risk

Relying only on price trains customers to wait for the next discount.

Practice · Brand Levels

19. The Smartphone Layers

Scenario

A new Pakistani phone brand launches with a mid-range handset. Competitors already offer similar cameras and batteries. The marketing team asks how to differentiate beyond the physical product.

Diagnosis

Competition is being fought only at the basic and expected levels.

Framework

Five product levels: Core benefit (main need, e.g. communication), Basic product (the phone itself), Expected product (battery, camera, screen), Augmented product (warranty, delivery, support, easy returns), Potential product (future improvements and experiences).

Recommended Moves

Compete at the augmented level first: strong local warranty, same-day delivery in major cities, easy doorstep exchange. Signal future potential through software updates and accessories.

Risk

Over-promising on service that the operations team cannot deliver will damage trust faster than a weak camera.

Practice · Buying Process

20. The Laptop Decision

Scenario

A university student’s old laptop dies two weeks before mid-terms. She reads reviews, asks friends, compares three models on battery and price, buys one online, then worries whether she chose correctly.

Diagnosis

She has moved through the full buying decision process under time pressure.

Framework

Five stages: 1) Need recognition 2) Information search 3) Evaluation of alternatives 4) Purchase decision 5) Post-purchase evaluation. Marketers can influence each stage.

Recommended Moves

Make comparison easy (clear specs), reduce risk at purchase (easy returns), and follow up after delivery to reduce dissonance.

Risk

Ignoring the post-purchase stage leaves buyers open to regret and negative word-of-mouth.

Practice · Purchase Factors

21. Bus or Careem?

Scenario

A young professional in Lahore must reach a client meeting across the city. The bus is cheap but slow and crowded. Careem is faster and more comfortable but costs several times more. She chooses Careem.

Diagnosis

She traded price for time, effort and quality.

Framework

Key factors affecting purchase: Prestige (status), Quality (reliability/comfort), Time (speed), Effort (physical/mental work), Price (money). People trade across these factors; price is rarely the only one.

Recommended Moves

For ride-hailing: emphasise time and effort savings for professionals. For bus operators: improve reliability and cleanliness to compete on quality, not just price.

Risk

Assuming every customer is purely price-driven leads to wrong offers.

Practice · Maslow

22. The Donation App

Scenario

A fintech startup launches an app that lets users donate automatically every time they order food. Early adopters are young professionals who already meet their basic needs and want to “give back” with minimal effort.

Diagnosis

The product speaks mainly to higher-order needs.

Framework

Maslow’s eight stages (or five in shorter versions): Physiological → Safety → Love/Belonging → Esteem → Cognitive → Aesthetic → Self-actualization → Transcendence. Products and messages should match the level the target customer is operating at.

Recommended Moves

Position the app around transcendence and ease (helping others without friction). Avoid heavy “save the world” messaging that feels like a chore.

Risk

Pitching transcendence to customers still struggling with physiological or safety needs will feel tone-deaf.
